# SSE ExEd Studio Control — Program-Wide UI/UX & Coherence Audit

_End-to-end, user-perspective scrutiny of the whole operator console, benchmarked to the freshly-polished **Audio Console**. Audit-only — **no application code was changed**. Every proposal is presentation-only, token-disciplined, holds across Studio / Graphite / Bone, and respects the no-scroll fixed-height posture at 2560×1440 (primary) and 1920×1080 (fallback). Proposals that would need new engine/protocol/OSC data are flagged and given a front-end-only partial instead._

_Produced 2026-06-05 by a 24-agent multi-pass workflow — 8 per-surface deep audits (source + rendered baselines + per-theme contrast) → 8 adversarial verifications → 6 cross-surface coherence lenses → master synthesis + completeness critic. **77 findings kept** (upheld or scope-corrected), **2 refuted** after verification._

---

## Executive summary

The Audio Console is genuinely gold-standard — it scores 86-100 across all six coherence lenses and sits ~30 points clear of the next surface. But that excellence is local, and the program's defining problem is that none of Audio's premium disciplines have been promoted to the shared layer. Every other surface re-derives chrome, theming, layout posture, control vocabulary, and feedback from scratch, so moving Audio→Lighting→Planning→Setup the operator watches the product visibly degrade: the canvas tint shifts neutral→green, the framing shifts dense-full-bleed→sparse-full-bleed→centered-gutter-with-dead-band, the controls invert (Cmd-drag means _fine_ in Audio but _coarse-x10_ in Lighting; double-click _opens typed entry_ in Audio but _resets_ in Lighting), and the same 'info' message renders amber, green, or grey depending on which surface you're on. It does not yet read as one product the moment you leave Audio.

Four SYSTEMIC gaps drive nearly every finding. (1) Theming is architecturally trapped in Audio — the entire Studio/Graphite/Bone system lives in AudioWorkspace's private `--bg`/`--fg` namespace, while the global `tokens.css` is a single dark `:root` with zero `[data-theme]` blocks, so dark chrome frames a near-white Bone audio body and the "three themes" promise breaks at the audio boundary. (2) The token foundation is incomplete even in the one dark theme that exists: DS primitives are wired to an undefined `--color-studio-*`/`--color-accent-*` namespace, Planning references undefined `--color-text-primary`/`--radius-xl`/`--font-size-xs`, and `--operator-font-size-title-xl` is referenced 3-4x with four different literal fallbacks — so shared severity labels render near-white, hierarchy collapses, and the biggest headlines freeze out of UI-scale. (3) Audio's premium disciplines aren't shared: full-bleed/full-height layout, container-query + studio-wall densification, tabular-nums telemetry, bespoke focus rings, role=slider keyboard operability, and typed numeric entry all stop at the Audio edge — leaving Planning with no keyboard reschedule and unfocusable cards, Setup/Startup with centered columns and ~600px dead bands, and Lighting/Planning with jittering digit columns. (4) The chrome/feedback contract is fragmented: Setup/Startup/Recovery bypass AppShellFrame entirely (no crest, tabs, monitor bar, health bar), the footer is built three incompatible ways, portaled palette/toast/dialog escape UI-scale and collide on z-index, no surface has an amber attention tone, raw internal enums ('connected', 'PROTOCOL_MISMATCH') leak as operator copy, and a degraded banner hardcodes a title that contradicts its own body. The encouraging part: the vast majority of the highest-leverage fixes are S-effort, presentation-only, additive token work — the program can close most of the coherence gap before the one genuinely large item (global theming) lands.

### Root causes (the systemic gaps)

1. Theming is trapped in Audio — global tokens have zero [data-theme], so dark chrome frames a Bone body
2. Incomplete token foundation — undefined studio/accent/text-primary/title-xl namespaces break severity, hierarchy and scale even in the one dark theme
3. Audio's premium disciplines aren't promoted — full-bleed density, tabular-nums, focus rings, keyboard operability and typed entry stop at the Audio edge
4. Fragmented chrome & feedback — Setup bypasses the frame, footer built three ways, portals escape UI-scale, no amber tone, enums leak as copy
5. Inverted control vocabulary — the same gesture (Cmd-drag, double-click) does opposite things on Audio vs Lighting
6. Three incompatible layout postures — full-bleed-dense vs full-bleed-sparse vs centered-gutter with dead bands

## Cross-surface coherence

This is the heart of the ask: not "is each screen good in isolation" but "does the program feel like one ultra-premium product as you move through it." Six lenses, each comparing all surfaces side by side against Audio.

### 1. Chrome & nav

Across the three primary workspaces (Audio, Lighting, Planning) the TOP chrome is genuinely coherent: all three render through AppShellFrame, sharing the same Crest ("EXECUTIVE EDUCATION / Studio Control"), NavItem workspace tabs (with correct aria-current="page"), and the three-pill monitor bar. The one-product feel collapses at three seams. (1) SETUP/STARTUP/RECOVERY bypass AppShellFrame entirely — OperatorShell returns a `.setupShell`/`.setupCanvas` branch (OperatorShell.tsx:322-446) BEFORE reaching `<AppShellFrame>` at L514, so Setup has no crest, no workspace tabs, no monitor bar, and a centered min(1400px) column with large side gutters + a tall empty bottom band (verified in setup-ready and protocol-mismatch baselines at both 2560 and 1920). Pressing ⇧S drops the operator out of the navigation chrome entirely. (2) The FOOTER/HEALTH BAR is implemented three incompatible ways: Audio hand-builds AudioHealthBar (no DS primitive, own module CSS), Lighting uses the DS HealthBar primitive (LightingHealthBar), Planning has NO bottom health bar at all (only a thin PROGRESS strip), and Setup has none — so the most-load-bearing piece of persistent chrome is present on 2 of 4 surfaces and built two different ways. (3) The PORTALED chrome (CommandPalette ⌘K, ShortcutOverlay ?, Toast stack, ShellDialog) all mount at document.body OUTSIDE OperatorLayoutProvider's `.root`, so they escape the 90/100/110/125 UI-scale and hardcode px; they also collide on z-index (palette 1200 == shortcut 1200; ShellDialog 30 < toasts 1000, so a modal renders below a non-modal toast); CommandPalette has zero visible focus ring (input outline:none, no :focus-visible); ShellDialog lacks the DS Dialog's focus-trap/Escape/restore; the Toast tone enum (ok|error|info) has no amber attention tone despite Audio's amber vocabulary. Cross-cutting: the three themes exist ONLY in Audio (data-audio-theme), so dark chrome frames a near-white Bone audio body; the shell carries two parallel token namespaces (AppShellFrame on --color-bg-_/--color-brand-text-_, OperatorShell.module.css on --color-surface-_/--color-text-_); and --operator-font-size-title-xl is undefined, referenced 4x across Setup-recovery + startup states + Planning with four DIFFERENT literal fallbacks (34/36/30/32px) that also bypass the UI-scale every other operator title honors.

**CHROME-03 — Command palette, shortcut overlay, toasts and dialog escape the UI-scale and collide on z-index; palette has no focus ring; ShellDialog lacks focus-trap/Escape** · Global chrome, Design system, Audio Console, Lighting, Planning, Setup / Support · high/M

- _Evidence._ --operator-font-size-\* and --operator-ui-scale-factor are defined ONLY on `.root` (OperatorLayoutProvider.module.css:21-32); CommandPalette, ShortcutOverlay, Toast and ShellDialog all portal to document.body (CSS header comments say so), OUTSIDE `.root`, so they ignore the 90/100/110/125 scale and hardcode px: CommandPalette.module.css uses 15px/14px/12px/10px font + pixel paddings (19 literal-bearing lines). Z-INDEX COLLISION: CommandPalette backdrop z-index:1200 (CommandPalette.module.css:14) == ShortcutOverlay backdrop z-index:1200 (ShortcutOverlay.module.css:13) — same plane, can co-occupy; ShellDialog overlay z-index:30 (OperatorShell.module.css:731) is BELOW toastStack z-index:1000 (toastStack.module.css:18), so a modal confirm renders beneath a non-modal toast. FOCUS: CommandPalette .input sets outline:none (L66) with zero :focus-visible in the file; active row is only `.itemFocused{background:var(--color-brand-green-soft)}` (L128-130). ShellDialog.tsx is a hand-built non-portalled modal with no focus trap, no Escape handler in the component (only the shell-level keydown), no focus restore, and a hardcoded id='shell-dialog-title'.
- _Proposal._ Presentation-only fixes: (a) add a z-index token scale (--z-toast/--z-dialog/--z-palette/--z-overlay) and re-layer so dialogs sit above toasts and the two lookup overlays don't share 1200; (b) give CommandPalette a visible :focus-visible ring on input + active row (reuse Audio's outline:2px solid var(--color-primary-400) pattern from AppShellFrame.module.css:148-151); (c) since the portals can't read `.root`, set --operator-ui-scale-factor on `document.documentElement` (or wrap the portal targets in a scale-bearing element) so palette/overlay/toast font sizes scale with the rest of the console — additive, no engine data; (d) port ShellDialog onto the DS Dialog primitive (focus-trap + Escape + restore) and drop the duplicated hardcoded id. All four are presentation-only.

**CHROME-01 — Setup/startup/recovery bypass AppShellFrame — no crest, no workspace tabs, no monitor bar; centered 1400px column breaks one-product nav** · Setup / Support, Startup / Recovery, Global chrome, Audio Console, Lighting, Planning · high/L

- _Evidence._ OperatorShell.tsx returns a `<div className={styles.setupShell}><div className={styles.setupCanvas}>` branch for every setup/startup/recovery experience (L322-341 startup, L362-392 ready-setup, L394-423 recovery-setup, L425-446 recovery) BEFORE the `<AppShellFrame>` at L514 — and AppShellFrame is rendered by exactly one caller (grep: only OperatorShell.tsx). `.setupCanvas` = width:min(1400px,100%); margin:0 auto (OperatorShell.module.css:267-272). Baselines setup-ready-2560x1440 and protocol-mismatch-2560x1440 show NO crest/tabs/monitor bar and a centered column with wide gutters, vs audio/lighting/planning-populated-2560x1440 which all carry the identical top chrome. An operator who presses ⇧S (OperatorShell.tsx:259-265) loses all navigation affordances and can only return via a small BACK TO CONSOLE text link.
- _Proposal._ Operator decision keeps OperatorShell 'double chrome' as-is, so do NOT force Setup through AppShellFrame. Presentation-only mitigations: (a) render a slimmed persistent top strip inside `.setupShell` that reuses the SAME Crest + NavItem workspace-tab row (the components already exist in @sse/design-system) so the crest/tab vocabulary and active-workspace affordance are continuous; (b) cap the centered column to the same near-neutral canvas tint as AppShellFrame (CHROME-05) and add `align-content` so it doesn't top-anchor (addresses the dead bottom band). Mark architecture_ok=true — this is pure JSX/CSS reuse of existing primitives, no state/engine change.

**CHROME-02 — Footer/health bar is built three incompatible ways (Audio hand-rolled, Lighting DS-primitive, Planning + Setup none)** · Audio Console, Lighting, Planning, Setup / Support, Global chrome, Design system · high/L

- _Evidence._ Audio mounts a bespoke AudioHealthBar (AudioWorkspace.tsx:745) whose component imports only lucide + its own AudioHealthBar.module.css — it does NOT use the DS HealthBar. Lighting mounts LightingHealthBar which DOES `import { HealthBar } from '@sse/design-system'` (LightingHealthBar.tsx:3,155). Planning has zero footer/health-bar code (grep for HealthBar/footerBar/statusBar over app/src/app/planning returns nothing) — planning-populated-1920x1080 and -2560x1440 show only a thin 'PROGRESS/overview' strip, no health bar. Setup surfaces have no health bar. So the single most persistent piece of operator chrome is present on 2/4 surfaces, rendered by two different code paths, and the DS HealthBar primitive (packages/design-system/src/components/HealthBar.tsx) is used by exactly one of them.
- _Proposal._ Converge on the DS HealthBar primitive as the single footer for all four surfaces: (a) reimplement AudioHealthBar's layout on top of DS HealthBar (Audio already defines the visual benchmark, so port its content into the shared primitive rather than the reverse — keep its testids); (b) add a HealthBar to Planning fed by the same buildMonitorItems-style health pills; (c) add the slim HealthBar to the Setup strip from CHROME-01. Presentation-only: HealthBar consumes already-available health snapshot data. If Audio's footer shows telemetry the DS HealthBar can't yet express, mark architecture_ok=false for that subset and ship the shared shell + Audio-specific slot.

**CHROME-04 — Three themes (Studio/Graphite/Bone) exist only in Audio — dark chrome frames a near-white Bone audio body; theme switcher is Audio-local** · Audio Console, Lighting, Planning, Setup / Support, Startup / Recovery, Global chrome, Design system · high/L

- _Evidence._ `data-audio-theme` is the ONLY theming hook in the app (grep across app/src: 4 hits, all in audio — AudioWorkspace.tsx:618 sets it on `.audioShell`, the child rendered INSIDE AppShellFrame; per-theme blocks AudioWorkspace.module.css:186 graphite + :236 bone). tokens.css is a single :root (packages/tokens/src/generated/tokens.css:5) with ZERO [data-theme]/.theme- overrides. The 'three themes' promise visibly stops at the audio-body boundary.
- _Proposal._ Promote the theme attribute from `.audioShell` to the shell root (set data-theme on the AppShellFrame `.shell` or documentElement) and migrate the graphite/bone overrides from AudioWorkspace.module.css into a shared themes.css keyed on [data-theme] against the --color-bg-_/--color-brand-text-_/--color-surface-_/--color-text-_ families. This is additive token work + moving an existing attribute up the tree — presentation-only, no engine data. Bone is the AA risk: re-verify monitor-pill text, NavItem, command-palette body and disabled rail captions in Bone (several already fail 3:1 per SET-10/STA-03).

**CHROME-06 — --operator-font-size-title-xl is undefined; 4 chrome/workspace headlines fall back to four different literals and bypass UI-scale** · Setup / Support, Startup / Recovery, Planning, Global chrome, Design system · medium/S

- _Evidence._ The operator scale defines title-sm(16)/title-md(18)/title-lg(22)/shell-title(30) (OperatorLayoutProvider.module.css:29-32) but NO title-xl. Yet --operator-font-size-title-xl is referenced 4x, each with a DIFFERENT hardcoded fallback: OperatorShell.module.css:418 (setupIncidentTitle, 34px), :622 (stateTitle, 36px), PlanningWorkspace.module.css:26 (30px) and :169 (32px). grep confirms it is defined nowhere in app/src or packages. So the largest headlines on the recovery surface, the startup state surfaces and the Planning header (a) render at inconsistent sizes across surfaces, and (b) are the ONLY operator titles that ignore --operator-ui-scale-factor (every defined title token multiplies by it).
- _Proposal._ Add --operator-font-size-title-xl: calc(34px \* var(--operator-ui-scale-factor)) to OperatorLayoutProvider.module.css:.root (pick one canonical size; 34 matches the recovery hero) so all four references resolve to one scaled value. Pure additive token — fixes both the size divergence and the UI-scale gap in one line.

**CHROME-05 — Canvas tint diverges per surface — green-tinted Planning + Setup vs near-neutral Audio/Lighting** · Planning, Setup / Support, Startup / Recovery, Audio Console, Lighting, Global chrome · medium/M

- _Evidence._ AppShellFrame `.shell` paints radial-gradient(var(--color-brand-green-glow)) + linear bg-deep→bg-canvas (AppShellFrame.module.css:11-13) — a restrained near-neutral with a faint green corner, as seen in audio/lighting baselines. Planning layers a stronger `radial-gradient(... var(--color-primary-500) 10% ...)` green wash on .planningShell (PLA-01) and Setup `.setupShell` layers primary-500 12% + warning-500 10% radials over surface-900→background-950 (OperatorShell.module.css:257-264) — both visibly greener than Audio in the 2560 baselines. ## Coverage notes — what a round 2 should add

From the completeness critic. This pass judged structure, source, rendered baselines, and computed contrast; the items below need runtime interaction or were deliberately out of this pass.

- **Graphite + Bone theme parity beyond Audio (lighting / planning / setup / global chrome / startup-recovery).** The light/dark theme switch is implemented ONLY for Audio: `data-audio-theme` lives on `.audioShell` (AudioWorkspace.tsx:618) with exactly two CSS overrides (AudioWorkspace.module.css:186 graphite, :236 bone), and the picker (AudioTopBar.tsx:10-11) is audio-local. No other workspace, the AppShellFrame chrome, the command palette, the ? overlay, toasts, context menus, or any dialog has a Bone/light variant. No Playwright spec renders or asserts graphite/bone anywhere — visual baselines capture only the implicit `studio` theme. So an operator who flips Audio to Bone gets a light mixer embedded in dark chrome/nav/inspector, and every shared overlay (palette, shortcuts, toast, ContextMenu) is unverified against a light backdrop. The audit's 'Graphite + Bone parity per surface' lens could not have been satisfied because the parity does not exist in code and was never screenshotted. _→ Round 2 should explicitly decide+document whether Bone/Graphite is intentionally Audio-only or a global theme. If global is intended, file it as a real gap (chrome, palette, ?-overlay, toast tones, dialogs, lighting/planning/setup all need light-theme tokens) and add per-theme visual baselines for at least one fixture per surface. If Audio-only is intentional, audit the seam where the light mixer meets dark chrome (top of AudioWorkspace, the AppShellFrame header, monitor rail) for contrast/legibility and write the rationale down so a future reviewer stops flagging it._
- **Runtime-only interaction states never captured in any baseline (hover / active / focus-visible / drag, open dropdowns, command-palette result list, ?-overlay, context menus, toast tones).** Every committed visual baseline is the at-rest 'populated/ready' state (visual-review.spec.ts FIXTURES). The lighting spec opens the overflow menu and inspector drawer but then re-navigates to capture the rest state (lines 273-274), so those overlays are functionally tested but never pixel-baselined. The CommandPalette result list + its `Recent` group + fuzzy-ranked groups (CommandPalette.tsx), the ShortcutOverlay search/filter/empty state (ShortcutOverlay.tsx), the ContextMenu danger-tone item + viewport-flip clamping (ContextMenu.tsx:58-73), the three Toast tones (ok/info/error `data-tone`, Toast.tsx:32) with the error-sticky vs auto-dismiss behavior, Tooltip hover+focus reveal, and all :hover/:active/:focus-visible states of buttons/faders/knobs/scene tiles are invisible to the static audit. These are the surfaces an operator actually touches during a live show. _→ Drive a real running shell (the Tauri dev shell or Playwright with explicit interaction) and screenshot each overlay OPEN: command palette (empty=Recent, typed=ranked, no-match), ?-overlay (default + filtered + empty), a danger context menu near a viewport edge (flip behavior), all three toast tones stacked, and hover/active/focus-visible on the primary control families. Consider committing a handful as new baselines so they stop being review-by-hand-only._
- **1920x1080 and 1280x800 density tiers + studio-preview fractional scaling judged structurally, not just by happy-path screenshot.** Only lighting has real density-fit assertions (`assertLightingResponsive` checks layout-mode, toolbar-control fit, stage min size, overflow/drawer behavior per tier, visual-review.spec.ts:126-204). Audio, planning, and setup get a screenshot + a generic no-scroll check (assertViewportFit) but NO structural assertion that their controls/labels actually fit or stay legible at 1920x1080 (studioFull lower bound) or the 1280x800 'narrowUtility' tier — the densest, most clipping-prone case. Separately, the Studio Preview is a uniform CSS `transform: scale()` at min(W/2560, H/1440) (OperatorLayoutProvider.tsx:157-160), ~0.59 on the 1512x982 review host; sub-pixel text hinting, 1px borders, hairline meter ticks, and icon crispness under that fractional scale are exactly what a static markup audit cannot evaluate, and the preview baseline only exists for setup/lighting/audio/planning — not for protocol-mismatch/recovery/degraded. _→ Add (or at least manually walk) per-surface fit assertions for audio/planning/setup at 1920x1080 and 1280x800 mirroring the lighting harness (no clipped controls, labels not truncated, dense rows readable). Inspect the audio mixer + inspector and the planning timeline at 1280x800 specifically. Eyeball the studio-preview at native fractional scale on the 14-inch review surface for text/tick crispness, and decide whether recovery/degraded states need a preview baseline too._
- **Loading / empty / degraded states per workspace (only one error fixture, all five base fixtures are fully populated).** The fixture set is setup-ready, protocol-mismatch, lighting-populated, audio-populated, planning-populated — i.e. one global startup error and four fully-populated happy paths. Empty-state and degraded-state code clearly exists across the app (grep shows empty/placeholder branches in AudioSnapshotDeck, AudioTieredMixer, SceneRail, LightingInspector, PlanningWorkspace, the inspector EQ/Sends/Dynamics tabs, etc.), and the shell has a per-workspace `degradedSummary` banner that is suppressed for lighting/audio/planning (OperatorShell.tsx:456-463) — meaning each of those workspaces is responsible for its OWN degraded presentation, which was never audited against a degraded fixture. First-run empty (no scenes / no fixtures / no projects / no snapshots), bridge-disconnected-mid-session, and partial-snapshot states are all unexamined. _→ Add empty and degraded fixtures (or URL params) for each workspace and audit them: zero-fixture lighting, zero-project planning, zero-snapshot audio deck, audio with OSC/TotalMix down, lighting with the bridge banner active, planning with no schedule. Verify empty-state copy, iconography, and that the in-workspace degraded affordance (since the shell banner is suppressed) is actually present and legible._
- **Keyboard focus order, focus return, and focus traps in the runtime overlays (palette, ?-overlay, context menu).** Only the shared Dialog.tsx implements a Tab focus trap + focus-first (lines 44-55). The three most-used runtime overlays do NOT trap Tab: ShortcutOverlay only handles Escape (ShortcutOverlay.tsx:177-183), CommandPalette handles arrows/Enter/Esc but a Tab press escapes the modal into the workspace behind it (CommandPalette.tsx:190-222), and ContextMenu handles arrow/Enter/Esc but not Tab. None of them restore focus to the triggering element on close (the palette/overlay/menu just unmount). For a keyboard-driven operator console this is a real accessibility + usability gap, and tab-order/focus-restoration is invisible to a static visual audit. The global Esc handler also has ordering rules (OperatorShell.tsx:226-244) that interact with these overlays and were likely not exercised by hand. _→ Round 2: tab through each overlay open and confirm focus stays inside and returns to the trigger on close; if not, add focus traps to CommandPalette/ShortcutOverlay/ContextMenu matching Dialog.tsx. Verify the Esc cascade (confirm dialog > shortcut overlay > palette > selection) does the right thing when multiple are stacked, and that ScrubSlider/knob keyboard nudge focus is reachable in tab order._
- **Reduced-motion for canvas/rAF-driven surfaces (CSS kill-switch does not stop JS animation loops).** global.css:74 has a broad `prefers-reduced-motion` rule that forces all CSS animation/transition to ~1ms — good for CSS, but it does NOT touch JavaScript requestAnimationFrame loops. The audio meters repaint ~30Hz on a canvas (AudioMeterCanvasOverlay), the AudioSignalCanvas paints peaks, the AudioArmCountdown animates, and the lighting StagePlot has JS-driven viewport/zoom motion (useStagePlotViewport.ts) and FixtureMarker pulse (FixtureMarker.tsx) — these continue full-motion under reduced-motion unless each loop explicitly checks the media query. A static audit reading CSS would conclude reduced-motion is 'handled' and miss that the most motion-heavy surfaces ignore it. There is a Playwright perf guard forbidding clip-path churn on the 30Hz meters (per MEMORY), which confirms these are live JS surfaces, not CSS. _→ Audit each rAF/canvas surface for a `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)')` branch: meters (does the operator-critical live meter keep updating but drop decorative easing?), countdown, find-pulse, fixture identify burst, stage-plot pan/zoom easing. Decide per-surface what 'reduced motion' should mean for a safety-relevant live meter (likely: keep the value, drop the smoothing/glow) and document it._
- **Iconography consistency across the three icon families (lucide vs custom planning icons vs fixture symbols) and across states.** The app mixes 29 lucide-react importers with two bespoke icon families: planning/icons.tsx and the lighting FixtureSymbol.tsx / FixtureSymbolKey. A cross-surface audit that treated icons per-finding could easily miss systemic inconsistency: stroke-width (lucide is drawn at strokeWidth 1.75 in several places, e.g. ContextMenu.tsx:209, Toast X at 2), sizing (13/14/16px appear inline), optical alignment of custom planning glyphs against lucide neighbors in the same toolbar, and whether the fixture-symbol key stays legible at the 1280x800 tier and under studio-preview downscale. Icon-in-context consistency is a whole-system property, not a per-screen one. _→ Do a dedicated icon pass: enumerate every icon source, normalize stroke-width/size conventions, check custom planning + fixture glyphs sit on the same optical grid as adjacent lucide icons, and verify icon legibility at the smallest density tier and under preview downscale. Flag any raster/SVG glyph that softens at ~0.59 scale._
- **Per-surface dialogs and sub-overlays examined individually (planning detail/time-report overlays, lighting create-fixture / rename / DMX-monitor / patch overlays, audio number/text/target dialogs).** The component inventory shows a large population of modal/overlay surfaces that are not part of any base fixture screenshot and are easy to under-sample in an 8-surface sweep: PlanningProjectDetailOverlay, PlanningTimeReportOverlay, CreateFixtureDialog, RenameDialog, DMXMonitorDialog, PatchOverlay/DMXPeek, AudioNumberDialog, AudioTextDialog, AudioTargetPicker, the audio inspector EQ band trays, and the lighting ColorPicker (used by InspectorGroup/InspectorScene/GroupChip/SceneTile). Each has its own header/footer/validation/empty/error presentation, focus behavior, and density behavior that only appears when invoked at runtime. A surface-level audit that screenshots the workspace-at-rest sees none of them. _→ Enumerate every dialog/overlay and open each one in a running shell: verify header/footer button hierarchy and copy, input validation + error styling, empty/invalid states, that they fit at 1280x800, that Esc/backdrop/Cancel all dismiss, and that they inherit (or break) the active theme. The ColorPicker and DMX monitor in particular deserve a focused interaction + a11y pass._

## Methodology & constraints respected

**How this was produced.** A deterministic multi-agent workflow ran 8 surface auditors (Lighting split into shell and stage/inspector; Planning; Setup + recovery; Startup/recovery; Global chrome; Design-system + tokens; and an Audio-residual reconciliation against audio's own 2026-06-02 audit). Each auditor viewed the committed rendered baselines, read the source TSX + every CSS module, computed WCAG contrast against the real theme tokens, and measured the surface against an 8-dimension gold-standard rubric. Every candidate then passed an independent adversarial reviewer that re-opened the cited files to refute it (dead-code checks, recomputed ratios, testid/no-scroll/settled-decision violations). Six coherence lenses compared all surfaces side by side; a synthesizer reconciled scores and prioritized; a critic named the coverage gaps.

**Constraints every proposal honors.** Architecture boundary (presentation-only, no state/persistence/device policy into React, no new engine/protocol/OSC data — partials given where the ideal fix would need it); no-scroll at 2560×1440 and 1920×1080; three themes (Studio/Graphite/Bone); additive token discipline; preserve testids; and the settled decisions — the retired multi-hue broadcast palette stays retired (single warm amber accent + clip-only danger red), intentional dead code (AudioRail, AudioToolbar, AudioPreampControl, AudioTargetPicker) is neither styled nor mounted, and the OperatorShell nav is left as the operator chose it.
